Cabinet Painting vs. Replacing Cabinets: Which Is Right for Your Kitchen?

Published October 11, 2026·By Cavalcante Painting Company

If your kitchen feels dated, the cabinets are usually the reason. They cover most of the room, so their color and finish set the tone for everything else. The good news is that you have two ways to fix it: paint the cabinets you have, or tear them out and install new ones.

Both can give you a kitchen you love. The right choice depends on the condition of your cabinets, your layout, your budget and how long you are willing to live without a kitchen. This guide walks you through the decision the same way we do when we visit a home for an estimate. If you already know painting is the route for you, you can read more about our cabinet painting in Toronto.

Start With the Cabinet Boxes

The most important question is not about color. It is about structure. Open a few doors and look at the cabinet boxes, the fixed frames attached to your walls.

Painting makes sense when:

  • The boxes are solid, square and firmly attached
  • The doors and drawer fronts are in good shape, with no warping or swelling
  • Drawers slide well and hinges hold, or only need simple hardware replaced
  • You like where everything is

Replacing makes more sense when:

  • There is water damage, swelling or mould, often under the sink
  • The boxes are sagging, cracked or coming away from the wall
  • Doors are badly warped or the surface is peeling away in sheets
  • The layout itself does not work for you

Paint changes how cabinets look. It does not fix cabinets that are falling apart. An honest painter will tell you when your cabinets are not worth painting.

Compare the Cost

Painting is almost always the less expensive option, usually by a wide margin. With new cabinets, you pay for the cabinets themselves, the removal of the old ones, the installation and, very often, new countertops, because old counters rarely survive being lifted off. Plumbing and electrical work can follow.

With painting, you pay for labour and materials: careful cleaning, sanding, priming and finish coats. Your counters, backsplash, plumbing and appliances stay exactly where they are.

Every kitchen is different, so we do not quote cabinet projects without seeing them. The number of doors and drawers, the material and the current condition all change the price. A free on-site visit gives you a real number to compare with a replacement quote.

Compare the Time and the Disruption

A full cabinet replacement is a renovation. Between ordering, delivery, demolition, installation and countertops, your kitchen can be out of use for weeks, and the schedule depends on several trades and suppliers.

A cabinet painting project is much shorter and far less disruptive. There is no demolition and no dust from tearing out walls or counters. In most cases you can still use parts of your kitchen while the work is underway, with some limits. You get a realistic timeline with your estimate so you can plan meals and routines around it.

Compare the Result

A new kitchen gives you full freedom: new layout, new door style, new storage. If you want to move the sink or add an island, paint cannot do that.

A professional paint job changes more than most people expect. With proper prep and a sprayed finish on the doors and drawer fronts, painted cabinets look smooth and even, not brushed over. Add new handles and the kitchen can feel completely different.

What paint will not change is the door style. If your doors are a flat slab and you dream of shaker doors, painting alone will not get you there. Some homeowners replace only the doors and drawer fronts and have everything painted to match, which sits between the two options.

How Long Does Each Option Last?

New cabinets with a factory finish are durable, and so are properly painted ones. The difference is in the prep. Kitchen cabinets collect grease, steam and fingerprints, and paint will not bond to a surface that has not been cleaned, deglossed, sanded and primed.

This is where do-it-yourself projects often fail. Paint applied straight over a glossy, greasy surface starts chipping at the edges within months. A professional process, with the right primer and a hard-wearing finish coat, is made for daily use. Like any finish, it should be treated with normal care: wipe spills, avoid harsh scrubbing pads and use the handles.

A Simple Way to Decide

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Are my cabinets structurally sound? If not, replace.
  2. Do I like my layout? If you want to move things around, replace. If the layout works, painting is on the table.
  3. What is my budget and timeline? If you want the biggest visual change for the least money and disruption, paint.

For many Toronto homes, the answers point to painting. The cabinets are solid wood or good-quality boxes, the layout works and the only real problem is a color that went out of style years ago.

Frequently Asked Questions

Painting is almost always cheaper, because you keep your cabinet boxes, countertops and plumbing in place and pay only for labour and materials. The exact difference depends on your kitchen, so compare a painting estimate with a replacement quote.

Most wood and MDF cabinets can be painted when they are in good condition. Laminate and thermofoil depend on the material and its state. We check this during the free visit and tell you what will work.

When doors and drawer fronts are sprayed after proper prep, the finish is smooth and even, without brush marks. That is the finish we aim for wherever spraying makes sense.
